1  •  What an Index Is

The weighting scheme is the measurement.

The Laspeyres Price Index

$$CPI_t = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{k} P_{t,i} \, Q_{0,i}}{\sum_{i=1}^{k} P_{0,i} \, Q_{0,i}} \times 100$$

Interpretation. It answers exactly one question: what would it cost today to buy what a typical consumer bought in the base year?

What freezing $Q_{0,i}$ gives you: comparability over time, simplicity.

What it costs: substitution bias, and it is upward.

Next slide: what that costs, in one picture.

What Freezing the Basket Costs

What the formula assumes

Same beef, at the new price, all year → measured cost up a lot

What households do

Switch to chicken → true cost up less

The gap = substitution bias

It is not a bug in the data. Freezing quantities gives comparability, and the price of comparability is a known, signed bias. Section 2 tries to correct it — Paasche bounds it, Fisher and Törnqvist split it, and Diewert says when the choice stops mattering.

The Answer, in Code

import pandas as pd

basket = pd.DataFrame(
    {"q0": [200, 600, 12], "p0": [3.00, 2.50, 1200], "p1": [4.20, 3.60, 1560]},
    index=["bread", "gasoline", "rent"])

cost_2024 = basket.p1 * basket.q0      # 840, 2160, 18720  → 21,720

cost_2020 / cost_2020.sum()            # share of the spending: rent 0.87
rise / rise.sum()                      # share of the rise:     rent 0.83

Three lines of output, and they are the three numbers on the slide before this one: 131.6, rent 87% of the spending, rent 83% of the rise. Same arithmetic you just did on paper.

What It Costs to Actually Build One

This is not a web scrape. When a sampled store closes, a person chooses the replacement outlet — same geography, similar demographics. When a laptop model is discontinued, that person decides whether the price gap is a price change or a quality change.

And the weights those prices are averaged with come from spending data about two years old.

The formula is the easy part — one ratio, and you did it on paper. Everything hard about the CPI is a judgement call someone made before the arithmetic starts.

Who Does the “Representative Consumer” Represent?

Housing is 36% of the CPI-U — and it is not house prices. A house is an investment; the CPI measures consumption, so it prices the shelter the house provides.

The OER premise: if you were renting your own house from yourself, what would you pay in the current market?

The BLS asks homeowners exactly that — and samples rents across existing tenancies, not just new leases.

Sampling Every Lease Means Measuring Late

Most tenants are mid-lease and paying last year's rent, so a spike in new-lease rents reaches the index only as those leases renew: the CPI's shelter component trails the market by 12–18 months.

The MBTA. Base fare \$1.70 (early 2012) → \$2.40 (2019), frozen since. 2012–24: +41% against CPI +37% — a ~3% real rise. But 2019–24 the CPI rose 23%, so the real fare fell ~18%. The window is part of the finding.

CPI-W and the experimental R-CPI-E exist as admissions that no single compression serves every user.

Four Questions Before You Trust an Index

1. What is in the basket?

CPI: 80,000 price quotes. Dow: 30 stocks.

CPI: spending shares. Dow: share price.

Recipients want high. Employers want low.

Poll: Does the Base Period Matter?

A colleague rebases the CPI to 2019 = 100 before deflating a wage series, instead of leaving it at 1982-84 = 100.

What happens to the real wage growth rate between 2019 and 2024?

A) It rises — a later base raises measured real growth
B) It falls — a later base lowers measured real growth
C) Nothing — rebasing is a constant rescaling
D) Undefined — you cannot deflate with a rebased index

Answer: C — Nothing Happens

Rebasing changes the units, not the growth rate. Rebasketing changes the weights — and the growth rate.

Rebasing multiplies the whole index by a constant, and a constant cancels in any ratio of two deflated values. The base period sets the units your real series is quoted in — “2020 dollars” — and nothing else.

Why A and B are tempting: in the Laspeyres formula the base period also fixes the quantity weights — and that does change the number. Two different jobs, one name: rebasing changes the units; rebasketing changes the weights. Only the second moves the growth rate.

Rebasing vs. Rebasketing: Why Nothing Happened

Rebasing

Change the unit of account. Every value is divided by the same constant — feet to meters.

Rebasketing

Change what is inside and how it is weighted. The ratios themselves change.

2  •  Which Index Answers Which Question

Four formulas. Four questions. One theorem that says when the choice stops mattering.

The Obvious Repair: Use Today's Basket

Paasche weights by today's quantities, so it understates; Laspeyres overstates. Neither is 'the correct one'.

$$P_t^{\text{Paasche}} = \frac{\sum_i P_{t,i} \, Q_{t,i}}{\sum_i P_{0,i} \, Q_{t,i}} \times 100$$

Interpretation. How much less would it have cost in the base year to buy what people buy today? A different question, not a better answer to the same one.

Laspeyres
overstates
frozen basket ignores substitution

Paasche
understates
today's quantities already embody the switching

Two indexes. Known, opposite biases. That is an opportunity.

Laspeyres · Paasche · Fisher · Törnqvist

Two Ways to Split the Difference

$$P_t^{\text{Fisher}} = \sqrt{P_t^{\text{Laspeyres}} \times P_t^{\text{Paasche}}}$$

Interpretation: the geometric mean of the upper and lower bound.

$$\ln P_t^{\text{T}} = \sum_{i} \tfrac{1}{2}\left(s_{0,i} + s_{t,i}\right) \ln\frac{P_{t,i}}{P_{0,i}}$$

Interpretation: each good's log price change, weighted by its average budget share across the two periods. The geometric analogue of Fisher.

$s_{0,i}$ and $s_{t,i}$ are good $i$'s share of total spending in the base and current periods. Where Fisher averages two indexes, Törnqvist averages two weighting schemes.

Write the Formula Once: def

import numpy as np
import pandas as pd

meat = pd.DataFrame(
    {"p0": [5.00, 3.00], "q0": [100, 100], "p1": [8.00, 3.60], "q1": [60, 150]},
    index=["beef", "chicken"])

def laspeyres(b):
    """Cost of the BASE-year basket at today's prices, base = 100."""
    return (b.p1 * b.q0).sum() / (b.p0 * b.q0).sum() * 100

def paasche(b):
    """Cost of TODAY'S basket at today's prices, base = 100."""
    return (b.p1 * b.q1).sum() / (b.p0 * b.q1).sum() * 100

def fisher(b):
    """Geometric mean of the two bounds."""
    return np.sqrt(laspeyres(b) * paasche(b))

laspeyres(meat), paasche(meat), fisher(meat)    # (145.0, 136.0, 140.43)
laspeyres(basket)                               # 131.6, the basket from earlier

Same table shape as the basket page: one row per good, columns p0 q0 p1 — plus q1, because Paasche needs today's quantities.

The last line is the point of def: the same function runs on the earlier basket and returns its 131.6. New data, no new code.

Diewert (1976): Superlative Indexes

Fisher and Törnqvist are both superlative: each is exact for a flexible cost function, so they agree to second order whatever the true preferences turn out to be.

Do not over-claim this. Superlative is a statement about approximation order, not about being right. The precise claim: a superlative index approximates any twice-differentiable homothetic cost function to second order.

It does not say the index equals the true cost-of-living index. It says the choice among superlative indexes stops mattering before the measurement error in the underlying price quotes does.

The Theory in Practice: the Chained CPI

Headline CPI-U is fixed-weight, not chained. The chained-CPI gap is about 0.27 pp a year.

C-CPI-U (published since 2002) is a Törnqvist, chained monthly with weights that move every month. Early prints use a CES approximation, revised 10–12 months later.

Measured on live FRED in Lab 2, Part 2:
≈ 0.27 pp / year

Quality Adjustment: Hedonic Regression

A laptop costs the same as last year and has twice the processing power. Did its price stay flat — or effectively fall?

$$\ln P_i = \alpha + \sum_{j=1}^{J} \beta_j z_{ij} + \epsilon_i$$

Interpretation. Regress log price on product characteristics. $\beta_j$ is the implicit price of one more unit of characteristic $j$ — screen size, processor speed, RAM. Rosen (1974).

$$\Delta P^{\text{adj}} = \left(P^{\text{new}} - P^{\text{old}}\right) - \sum_j \hat\beta_j \left(z^{\text{new}}_j - z^{\text{old}}_j\right)$$

Where Quality Adjustment Breaks

The laptop is the textbook case — and a rounding error in the index. Most of the weight that gets a hedonic correction is housing, adjusted for the ageing of rental units.

Medical care
A drug costs 5× more and adds two years of life. Price increase, or quality gain? No agreed method.

Housing
Rent and OER get an age adjustment. Renovations are ubiquitous and heterogeneous, and there is no characteristic list for them.

Free digital goods
Zero price. The index is defined on prices, so it cannot see them at all.

Free-Good Problem: Example Answers

Prompt A — price cut or quality gain?

Common: “A price cut, \$50 to \$0.”

Better: “A quality gain — hedonics should catch it.” Only if a priced item replaces a priced item.

Strong: “Neither. The \$50 product leaves the sample and the free app never enters: nothing is paid, so it has no price and no expenditure weight. What was gained is consumer surplus, and the CPI has no slot for it.”

Prompt B — what would you measure?

Common: “Ask what people would pay.” Anchored at zero, and capped by income.

Strong: a willingness-to-accept choice experiment — “Give up search for a year for \$X?” with X randomised across respondents, so each answer is a real either/or choice rather than a number someone makes up.

Brynjolfsson, Collis & Eggers (PNAS 2019), median to give up for a year:

Search \$17,530  ·  Email \$8,414  ·  Digital maps \$3,648

That number is a welfare measure, not a price: it can enter an adjusted GDP, never the CPI. Stated preference also inherits the usual costs — hypothetical answers, and WTA above WTP.

The Operation You Already Know

$$\text{Real Value}_t = \frac{\text{Nominal Value}_t}{CPI_t} \times 100$$

Interpretation. Divide by the price level to express purchasing power in constant units. Multiplying by 100 rescales into base-period dollars.

\$5,000 in 1970
CPI 38.8 → roughly \$40,400 in 2024 dollars.

Against \$55,000 today that is a 36% real gain — not the 1,000% the nominal comparison suggests.

The gap between 1,000% and 36% is money illusion.

4  •  Goodhart as Measurement Design

Not a cautionary tale. A design constraint.

Goodhart's Law

Goodhart: a statistical regularity collapses once pressure is placed on it for control purposes.

“Any observed statistical regularity will tend to collapse once pressure is placed upon it for control purposes.”

Charles Goodhart, 1975, on UK monetary aggregates that stopped predicting once policy leaned on them. The version everyone quotes — “when a measure becomes a target, it ceases to be a good measure” — is Marilyn Strathern's, 1997.

The issue is optimisation, not dishonesty. Agents optimise the measured proxy, not the concept — and the two come apart.

Every KPI Is an Index

DAU / MAU — the “stickiness ratio.” Facebook's ran around 0.65, best in class. But what counts as “active”?

It measures frequency. Never quality.

$$LTV = \frac{ARPU}{\text{monthly churn rate}}$$

LTV = lifetime value of a customer  ·  ARPU = average revenue per user, per month

Interpretation. \$12.99/month at 5% monthly churn implies \$259.80 of expected lifetime revenue. It assumes churn and ARPU are constant — and both are false. Churn is front-loaded.

The catch: teams improve “LTV” by redefining churn — a paused subscription counted as active — rather than by retaining anyone.

Three Mechanisms — the Fix Depends on Which You Have

1 · Optimisation
against the measure

The index has fiscal consequences, so its construction comes under pressure. CPI ↔ COLAs, TIPS, tax brackets.

Fix: institutional independence; publish the method

2 · Gaming
the measure

Agents inflate the number without improving the thing. Meta's notification tuning. The UK's REF (Research Excellence Framework, the audit that sets university research funding): star researchers hired just before the census date, so their papers count for the new school.

Fix: counter-metrics

3 · Obsolescence
of the measure

The basket stops matching the world. Gym memberships still weighted during lockdown; home office equipment underweighted.

Fix: reweight more often; population-specific indexes

Meta's MSI: the Counter-Metric That Was Goodharted

January 2018 — the repair

Meta re-ranks News Feed around Meaningful Social Interactions, explicitly to move off passive consumption toward real interaction between people.

Exactly what the theory prescribes: a counter-metric.

2021 — the second failure

MSI weighted reshares and comment threads heavily. The ranking system learned the reliable route to “meaningful interaction” is content that provokes argument.

Internal research found it amplifying divisive material. Meta's own data scientists had flagged it.

A counter-metric is still a metric. It compresses, it becomes a target, and it can be optimised into a failure of its own.

Index repair is maintenance you keep doing, not a state you reach.

Poll: What Does MSI Prove?

Meta built MSI as the counter-metric to passive consumption — and MSI was Goodharted in turn.

What follows for measurement design?

A) Counter-metrics do not work; rely on qualitative judgement instead
B) A counter-metric is itself a metric — it needs its own guardrail; repair is maintenance, not a terminal state
C) The failure was the wrong primary metric; a better North Star avoids this
D) Deploy many counter-metrics at once so no single one can be gamed

Answer: B — Repair Is Maintenance

Why A is wrong: the counter-metric did work — it detected and corrected the original passive-consumption problem. It then acquired a gaming surface of its own.

Why D is tempting: more metrics do raise the cost of gaming. But every metric you add is another target, and a system optimising against ten proxies is not obviously better aligned than one optimising against two. You have multiplied the surface, not removed it.

The Vocabulary to Leave With

North Star

The one metric best proxying delivered value. Spotify: time listening. Airbnb: nights booked. Choosing it is as consequential as the BLS choosing the basket.

Guardrail

Must not degrade while the North Star is optimised. If “time listening” rises because autoplay queues unwanted songs, “skips within 10 seconds” should catch it.

Counter-metric

Built specifically to detect gaming — and, per MSI, itself gameable. The immune response, not immunity.

Metric tree

$\text{Revenue} = \text{Users} \times \text{Conversion} \times \text{AOV}$, where AOV is average order value, revenue per order. Decomposition makes explicit which sub-metrics drive the aggregate — and which are ignored.

Key Takeaways

An index is a compression algorithm. Compression always discards. Ask what it discarded and whether that matters for your question.

Laspeyres and Paasche bound the answer. Our wedge: 9.0 points, with Fisher and Törnqvist 0.05 apart. Superlative is a claim about approximation order, not truth.

The deflator is part of the finding. “Real wages were flat for fifty years” is a joint claim about wages and the CPI. At ~0.27 pp/yr, compounding to ~15% over 53 years, the choice of ruler matters.

Goodhart is a design constraint. Diagnose the mechanism — optimisation, gaming, or obsolescence — because the fix differs for each.

The counter-metric is not immunity. MSI was the repair, and it was Goodharted. Measurement is maintenance — and you have to ask “what did this discard?” of the guardrail too.
